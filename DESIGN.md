# Apex Out Run — Coast 2 Coast Design System

The visual identity is **cinematic golden-hour simcade**: the Stitch "Coast 2 Coast" frame ([`journal_assets/stitch_outrun_vision.png`](journal_assets/stitch_outrun_vision.png)) is the target. A red wedge supercar seen from a low chase camera, a wide multi-lane highway running into haze toward a low sun, and dense roadside silhouettes. Glass telemetry panels sit at the corners and never cover the road.

## Every stage

- **Road**: four lanes, white dashed lane lines, solid white edge lines, a rumble strip at the off-road edge.
- **Light**: one sun disc on or near the horizon the road runs toward, a sun-tinted directional light, and an ambient light biased toward the sky colour.
- **Depth**: exponential fog in the painted sky's own horizon colour (sampled when the sky is painted), so silhouettes fade into the light rather than popping in.
- **Density**: signature props repeat every few segments on both sides. A stage reads by its silhouettes before its colours.

## Scenery

The art pack (`assets/README.md`) dresses each stage; what it lacks is generated in the browser at stage load, in real 3D.

- **Sky**: a painted dome per stage, the stage's photographed HDR sky (CC0, Poly Haven) repainted by SDXL so it keeps its sun, clouds and horizon; the night city's starts from a drawn skyline instead, since its photograph was taken among the towers and their verticals leaned on the dome. The dome undoes the tone curve and exposure, so the painting lands on screen as painted. The photographed sky still lights the scene, its sun handed back as the directional light that casts the shadows; an overcast or night sky has none.
- **Toon** (the owner's chosen illustration, on every stage): the lit colour of every surface is replaced by two soft bands of the light reaching it, cast shadows included, painted-miniature light rather than a hard cel: lit tones toward the sun's colour warmed to white, shade toward the sky's own light, against the sun's full strength, so the backlit stage falls mostly into shade and reads as silhouettes. A warm rim catches upright silhouettes facing the sun, never ground seen at a grazing angle. Every sky with a sun turns so a low sun disc stands just right of the road ahead, with a soft halo; an overcast or night sky stays as painted. Lamps keep their glow and the fog is laid back on. The frame's own banding is gone, since the materials band the light; the ink is a light pencil line, since a layout under studio light has no outlines of its own.
- **Stylisation**: one pass over the whole frame inks the silhouettes where the depth breaks, the ink thinning with distance as the fog does, anti-aliased after it so outlines are lines, not pixel stairs, and pushes the colour, so every model and surface reads as one painted world. The frame is drawn at the display's own pixel density, up to two pixels per CSS pixel, and nothing sharpens it.
- **Horizon**: the distance is painted, as the Ghibli concepts' is: two painted rings stand about the camera as the sky does, a far one 2.4 km out and 520 m tall and a mid one 1.2 km out and 190 m tall, each tiling in whole tiles four heights wide so its paint keeps its aspect, its silhouette the strip's alpha. The mid ring sinks below the land where the road runs ahead and behind, so the road is never walled off, and over the water, so no treeline stands in the sea; the far one stands all round, the far shore across the water. Strips laid along the road were tried first: driving down it they showed only as a sliver at the vanishing point. Rings 1 to 2 km out move under a degree a second at speed, so they carry no parallax of their own; the 3D props nearer carry the motion. Each stage paints its own distance (`assets/tools/ghibli/backdrops`): coastal headlands over a palm line for the coast, red buttes for the canyons and the sunset road, a soft rounded snow range with violet shade over a fir ridge for the alpine pass (a range of cut triangles read as geometry) (it replaces the 3D snow peaks, flat cones that read as cut-outs; the alpine firs stand back from the road and thinner, so the range shows over them), windmills with their sails over flat polder for the tulips, a lit skyline for the city, olive hills and cypress for the ruins, and so on; each a procedural silhouette, periodic so the strip tiles, filled with the land's colours over its sky's horizon colour, then painted as Oga gouache by SDXL with circular padding. Aerial perspective is baked in (the far strip mixed halfway toward its sky's horizon, the mid a fifth), and the game draws them as it draws the sky, undoing ACES and the exposure, so they sit in their sky as painted.
- **Water**: the sea carries a generated wave normal map that drifts over time and a sun-tinted glitter. A foam line marks the shore.
- **Ghibli gouache** (on branch `feat/apex-ghibli`, see Review; the model railway stays on `feat/apex-outrun`): the world reads as a Kazuo Oga background driven through on a painted afternoon. The skies are Oga's: each stage's photographed sky repainted as opaque gouache with towering cumulus over a clear afternoon gradient whatever the photo's own light (the concepts' clear blue; the sunset road's warm), its brightest parts becoming the cream cumulus, warm-topped and blue-grey bellied, the night city's as painted stars over its skyline (`assets/tools/ghibli/skies`). Over the road's horizon, painted cumulus stand in three banks one behind another, as the concepts' skies do (one ring of cards pinned to the camera read as a flat backdrop): fourteen cards, 1.3, 2.2 and 3.4 km out to either side of the road, 380 to 950 m tall and taller the further the bank, their bases floating 140 to 340 m above the eye, the further banks fading toward the haze. Each card is anchored in the world along the road's run, so the car drives past it and a near bank slides by faster than a far one; all drift on the wind at 1.2 m/s and sway across it, and a card a quarter of its bank's span behind comes round ahead again. Each card is its own (`cumulusCard`): seven kinds, a towering cumulus, a wide low bank with a rising heap, twin towers, a rounded puff, an anvil whose cap shears downwind, a ridge of humps climbing upwind, a narrow spire, each its own proportions and every heap trailing a low skirt downwind; a stage's cards take the kinds in turn, so each bank's are all different and each kind serves two banks, its second card mirrored (the owner: "all the clouds looking the same is not great either, specially given they are shown together"); each lobes heaped on a flat base, soft-edged and painted in two soft steps from a deep blue-grey belly through a body held under white to a cream sunlit top (bodies at white went flat through the tone curve and read as cut-outs), drawn under the bloom; none on a sunless sky. The dome writes no depth, so the far bank stands beyond it. The dome has no seam: of two longitudes half a turn apart, each pixel samples the one whose derivative is small, over a sky that repeats around it (a seam column sampled the smallest mip and flashed as a thin line down the frame). The ground is painted, not built, bright and warm as the concept's earth: grass, beach sand, desert sand, forest floor, field soil, granite, ruins stone and the road are painted from palettes, flat gouache washes cut from broad noise pushed by brush strokes so every region's edge is a stroke (`assets/tools/ghibli/textures/flat.py`), the desert floor a warm red-orange earth, held back from orange sand (t29's olive read as drab beside the concept's red earth), the sunset road's share of it tinted cooler toward its dusk, the grass and forest floor lighter, held-back greens (measured against the concepts' land, luminance 0.27 and saturation 0.38, every green stage's land sat at saturation 0.5 to 0.6), the coast pale and the road a dark warm wash (the orange sand and bright greens measured twice the concepts' saturation); snow, cobble and canyon rock are SDXL gouache repaints of their cartoon sets (`paint.py`); the sets tile every 9 m, they tile every 9 m so their brushwork reads at driving distance, and the ground's colour comes in patches tens of metres across, lighter and darker. Every model is painted too, the props, the riders and the cars' trim alike, in world space since the models have no UVs: one tile of gouache strokes (`brushStrokes`, slightly bent bands with tapered ends and bristle streaks, leaning one way as a painter's hand does) is laid on the three planes each surface faces, a tile every 1.25 m out to 12 m and twice as wide with each doubling of distance past it, blended across the two nearest sizes, so a stroke keeps its size on screen instead of averaging to grey at driving distance; lighter strokes warm and darker ones cool, each pushed hard toward its end; each model warms toward the sky and cools over its lowest 2 m, and its contour darkens softly where it turns away from the eye (the owner chose the concepts' painted models over the noise mottle, which read as dirty plastic). The ground and the road carry painted sets of their own and no strokes, and the cars' lacquer stays lacquer. Canopies, bushes and palm crowns are also painted as clustered leaf masses about a metre across, each a cell lit warm at its heart with dark between the masses, split between a warm lit tint and a cool blue-green shade (noise blotches read as lumps) (canopies are also built as painted leaf masses, pushed in and out along their normals in masses a third of their size across, with warm light and cool blue-green shade baked into their colour). The biggest on-screen props carry painted surfaces baked by projection (`assets/tools/ghibli/bake`): the desert's buttes, the palms, the firs, cypresses and broadleaf trees, the sea stacks, the columns and the broken arches (the castle, colosseum and hangar keep the pack textures they carry) are rendered from five views, each repainted as Oga gouache by SDXL at a strength that keeps the silhouette, and what each painting adds to its render, its brushwork and gently its warm and cool shifts, is projected back over the model's own colour into one UV atlas per LOD (xatlas), each texel taking the views that see it face on (taking the paintings' colours whole met in patchwork, and a palm's trunk took its fronds' green). Painted sprite cards were tried and not adopted (local branch `proto/t33-painted-sprites`): each of eight azimuths' painting drawn whole as a card past 24-40 m, crossfading between the nearest views; at chase distance the palms read the same as the baked models and the buttes lighter and flatter, unlit by the stage, no closer to the concepts. A painted material marks itself in its glTF extras; on it the toon light only modulates (its darkest band keeps half the sun) and no strokes or leaf cells are laid over the painting. The props are painted-world shapes: the desert's buttes are tall warm red rock, 55 m and more, deeply fluted from top to bottom on faceted plans under rounded shoulders, one model a cluster of three spires (`assets/tools/props/landforms.py`), with no horizontal strata (cream and orange bands round squat cylinders read as banded drums), the near ones 38 m and more off the road so they frame it as the concept's do and the far ones 160 m and more, the painted distance showing between them; the desert's near rocks are few and small, its verge dense low green scrub, and the pack's saguaros, 4.6 m with two arms and repainted a muted sage under warm light (the pack's toy green read bright beside the concept's), and the pack's cypress and broadleaf foliage is lifted toward the concepts' lit greens (their stages' land measured luminance 0.11-0.16 against the concepts' 0.27), stand along the road every segment; the coast's sea stacks are rounded, fluted grey rock under a domed grass cap (stacked chamfered blocks with an ochre tier read as the cartoon pack), and the gateway a stone arch of voussoirs on plinthed pillars in grey-cream limestone (warm limestone read mauve in the light) (it was a purple slab of boxes); the palms stand 14 m, tall and slender with long drooping fronds, and the firs 15 m in six and seven layered tiers of deep green (`assets/tools/cartoon/plants`). The light falls in three soft painted steps, high-key, lit tones warm and shade a deep blue-green, from an afternoon sun behind the viewer and to the left; the air is clear, each stage's haze at 22%. The grade is a clear afternoon's: soft cream highlights over cool blue shade, colour not pushed at all (1.0) and contrast low, since the concepts are luminous and a little held back; the golden-hour grade's orange highlights had turned every sky olive. The darks are true darks: the road is charcoal, its painted wash drawn at a third of the old albedo, and the toon shade is deeper; t29's darkest tenth sat at twice the concepts' black level, a grey veil over the whole frame. The road's centre line is yellow between white lane lines and edges, as the concept's. Coconut Beach's road runs beside a coast promenade, as the concept's does: a painted mid-grey pavement 3.6 m wide (pale paving read as sand) from the rumble edge to a low painted sea wall 0.9 m tall and 0.6 m thick, the sea beyond it; palms grow out of the promenade and a bed of bushes and flowers runs along the wall's foot, as the concept's greenery stands against its wall, but no other verge scatter covers it. The coast's land side is green verge, not pale sand, as the concept's is. The frame is gouache on paper: its colour gently quantised toward flat washes over a paper grain two pixels across, with only a soft edge left of the ink, and no blur at all. The verges are green and flowered but low and sparse, so the palms, the buttes and the fields read over them (`shell/engine/scatter.js`): low scrub as the concepts paint it, textured rather than rounded: bushes of nine sprigs leaning out from the root, at most 1.1 m, grass tufts of fanned blades and wildflower patches of heads at a few heights, in each stage's own colours (four-lobed bushes and round mounds read as cotton balls), at most 1.3 items a metre a side, most within 5 m of the road and thinning out to 25 m, never on the asphalt, in a prop or by the flagman; dry stages grow sage bushes with cacti; Tulip Gardens grows no bushes before its field, only low grass and dense painted poppies, orange and red heads up to the asphalt as in the concept, before its rows of red, pink and coral painted as soft masses between bands of leaf (pure-pigment stripes of single heads read as a model's), under a power line along the road, its three wires sagging from pole to pole, with slim white turbines far off beside the windmills; the poppies' verge is painted as a flower carpet 0.4 to 6 m from the road, orange and red heads crowded edge to edge, so the flowers read as one mass at speed where instanced heads read as dots, the heads standing on it near the camera; the alpine verge is soft continuous snow banks, low mounds strung along the road (round snow clumps read as cotton balls); the city's verges are pavement and carry none (lush banks of 2.4 m bushes hid the palms and the tulips). Each kind is one mesh's thin instances per 300 m chunk, and only the chunks from 30 m behind the camera to 360 m ahead draw. The cars keep their lacquer: paint, chrome, rims and glass lit as themselves rather than painted (deep clear-coated paint, mirror chrome reflecting the probe that follows the car), and each car carries the pack's two sculpted Ghibli riders (`model/riders`, `assets/tools/cartoon/riders`: SDF figures meshed by the flagman's toon pipeline, with baked occlusion), riding the sprung body: the girl drives from the left seat, both hands on the wheel (an auburn bob in shaped locks with long locks streaming back, a red ribbon, a yellow dress with a round white collar, puffed sleeves), and the boy rides beside her (messy dark tufts, a blue jacket with a turned-up collar and placket, a red neckerchief whose tail streams back); both have big low-set dark eyes, brows, a button nose, a small mouth and rosy cheeks. Her long hair and ribbon and his neckerchief are nodes of their own that turn about their pivots on the wind time, faster with speed. He rides with his outer arm on the door, the forearm along its top and the hand gripping its outer edge, fingers curled over the outside (an arm hung out over the door read as one a passing truck would take off), and waves only at a moment that earns it, the start, a close pass, beating the rival, a checkpoint or nitro's kick: the sim's wave timer runs 75 ticks (1.25 s), a waving arm of its own takes the resting one's place and lifts, waves and lowers, eased; and every 9 s his head glances toward the scenery or the girl, in turn (the owner: "the guy waving non-stop makes no sense"). All of it comes from the state, so a replay poses them alike; the player's car carries the full riders, the others the light ones.
- **Ground and road**: hand-painted PBR sets per stage (sand, snow, forest floor, cobbles, soil, grass) and worn asphalt, tiled through the meshes' own UVs. SDXL repainted each from a CC0 photoscan with its grain flattened first and its average colour kept, so the stage lighting still fits; grass alone moved to a cartoon meadow green.
- **Zones**: every route stage runs through three or four scenes rather than one mix from start to finish: Coconut Beach's palm avenue, open beach, marina with its lighthouse, and headland; Desert Canyons' cactus plain, canyon of close mesas, and wide mesa valley; Gateway Pass's cypress avenue under the arches, open hills, and rocky pass; and so on. A prop spec holds to its zone, a span of the stage. Every stage has one hero landmark of its own, a painted model placed once where the camera's moment frames it (the owner: "I think we had this idea that each scenario had a unique landmark? I dont see them"): Coconut Beach's lighthouse on the marina's point, half again the size of a common one; Desert Canyons' great rock arch, a thin red span from a tall fluted butte to a lower one; Gateway Pass's Tuscan chapel and campanile on a grass knoll; Alpine Peaks' white ridge chapel under a copper onion dome; Wilderness Run's great tree alone in the meadow, 30 m; Tulip Gardens' great windmill across the field from the row, 40 m; Sunset Highway's lone butte against the dusk; Ancient Ruins' aqueduct striding along the grass hills, its end fallen; Metropolis Night's spire over the waterfront, 150 m; Coastal Cliffs' sea arch at the water's edge; the marina's harbour clock tower, the oasis's kasbah, the castle's keep, the colosseum and the aerodrome's great airship hangar (`assets/tools/props/landmarks.py`: SDF solids meshed by marching cubes, then baked painted as the other big props). The camera's rarer moments also land on each zone's set pieces (mesas, cliffs, windmills, broken arches, towers), never within 400 m of the start nor 900 m of another moment.
- **Props**: where the pack has a model for a prop kind, it stands in at the same placements and collisions, each placement drawn at full, quarter or one-sixteenth detail by distance. Every kind but the gateway arch, dunes, the oasis pool and runway lights has one: palms, rocks, mesas, cliffs, firs, broadleaf trees, cypresses, shrubs, grass, cacti, columns, broken arches, power poles, lit towers and street lamps, windmills whose sails turn, a lighthouse, sailboats, castle towers, the colosseum at its real 48 m, and hangars with their doors to the road. Rocks, the cactus, the columns and the power poles are procedural cartoon solids too, built from signed distance fields with flat faces and rounded edges. Rocks come in three stones, each a set of six or fewer distinct shapes (low slabs, domed boulders, tall stacks, split blocks): banded sandstone in the desert stages, grey-blue granite with lichen in the mountains and on the coast, pale limestone at the ruins and the castle. A stage's set is scaled as one, so it averages 1.4 m and its shapes keep their own proportions, from a knee-high slab to a stack twice a person's height; every set's widest boulder at its largest placement stays off the asphalt. Vegetation is procedural cartoon geometry: puffy canopies and tiered conifers, solid leaves and fronds rather than photo cards, and the wind moves fronds, blades and leaves. Every model carries ambient occlusion baked into its vertex colours, soft light that costs nothing per frame. Lamps join the glow; tower windows are lit cells over dark glass. Mesas, buttes and sea cliffs are closed, stylised solids with bevelled facets and painted strata: the photoscans they replaced were single rock faces, open at the back, and read as shells from the side. A large prop (mesa, cliff, colosseum, hangar, tower, castle tower, lighthouse) stands on the lowest ground under its footprint, across the road and along it, so where the land falls away or the road climbs its base sinks into the slope instead of hanging over it with its underside in view.
- **Tulip field**: Tulip Gardens' ground is the field itself, stripes 4 m wide of red, yellow, pink and orange bloom between soil furrows, tiled over 16 m, growing right up to the asphalt's edge and never on it; a 6 m grass verge between them was tried and made the field poorer. Beds standing beside the road read as obstacles.
- **Night city**: Metropolis Night is lit by its photo sky at a third of its strength, since that photo is a street among lit shopfronts; at full strength it floodlit every tower wall.
- **Fork gantry**: before the lane split, a gantry board over each lane names the stage that branch leads to, white on the branch's colour, cyan left and orange right.
- **Traffic**: the pack's 80s sedan, convertible, pickup and box truck, each car in its own paint, pitched on its own axles.
- **Scatter**: rocks, shrubs and grass tufts fill the ground between the signature props on the stages where they belong.
- **Light**: on WebGPU, the stage's own sky captured into an environment map lights the PBR car paint (clear-coated), glass and metals. Three-cascade sun shadows fall from palms, props and cars. The sun throws shafts through the haze, and a colour grade warms the highlights and cools the shadows, under 4× MSAA plus FXAA and a soft bloom. WebGL2 gets the same, minus the reflections and MSAA and with smaller shadow maps. There is no filmic tone mapping.

## Radio and sound

All sound is synthesized live with WebAudio; the app ships no audio files. The engine note follows RPM and gear, wind rises with speed, tyres squeal in a drift, and each race event has its own cue. The mix is a Jessie sound role (`program.cue` `surface.engines.sound`) that holds one AudioContext the terminal hands it, tamed of worklets and other output devices, so sound is a view of the race like pixels and reaches nothing else; its noise bed is drawn from a fixed seed, the same every launch.

The radio is the glass HUD tuner (chosen on 2026-10-16 over an in-dash amber LCD): live equalizer bars driven by the mix itself, the frequency tag, the station name large with its genre beneath, and NEXT and MUTE buttons at least 44 px tall. Off, it reads "RADIO OFF · ENGINE ONLY". The title card offers the three stations and a volume slider before the start.

## End of a run

A run ends at a destination (GOAL!, with the ending's letter and name) or when the clock runs out (TIME OVER, with the stage reached). A time over holds its TIME OVER for a beat and goes to the results. A goal is three moments, the HUD's panels stepping aside for the first two (the owner: "finishing the last stage still drives the car into the void"):

- **The arrival.** GOAL! flashes under the final arch, and the road runs on into the ending's own place: Marina Bay's harbour clock tower, Desert Oasis's kasbah, the Grand Castle's keep between two gate towers, the Colosseum, the Aerodrome's great airship hangar, each standing over the road's end 245 to 300 m past the arch (`STAGE_LOOKS` `destination`), the last stretch's scenery mirrored along the way and land 600 m on behind it. The car drives itself in, as the checkpoint's autopilot does: it eases to a cruise, brakes and pulls up 150 m past the arch, short of the place. The camera's landmark pull frames the place over the car, the haze stays back, and once it stops the boy waves and the girl lifts her outer hand from the wheel and waves. Eight seconds, all of it the simulation's, so a scrub frames it alike.
- **Your road trip.** A scenic replay of the player's own run, rebuilt from the run's rows (pronto's kinetic-machine record), not an AI driver: seven seconds of each stage raced, opening 360 m short of its hero landmark, the race's own frames on its own commands. Each window is cut in three: a helicopter high and wide behind the car sliding up alongside it, the chase camera's own landmark pull, and a camera at the roadside opposite the landmark that waits for the car and turns to follow it by, its lens narrowing while the car is far. It is letterboxed, YOUR ROAD TRIP over the first window and each window's stage card over it, the radio playing on: about 14 s for two stages, 35 s for five. Enter or the gamepad's confirm skips it.
- **The results**, over the car parked at the place.

Both cards show the same score breakdown, then three arcade initials when the run makes the top 10: ▲▼ or a typed letter changes the slot, ◀▶ or Backspace moves, Enter saves. A gamepad's stick and pad work the same way. Once the race has ended the keys are the initials', never the car's. Escape or P pauses: the race holds under the pause card while the radio plays on.

| Line | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Distance | 10 per metre driven |
| Close passes | 50 each |
| Rivals beaten | 1,000 each |
| Time left at a goal | 1,000 per second |

The high-score table is one shared online top 10 for every player, showing rank, initials, ending, time and score, with the player's new entry highlighted in the sun's gold.

A duel's end card is unchanged: a duel lifts no landmark milestones, so it has no road trip.

## Stage change

The road never ends on screen. A fork stage's two branches run on 400 m past the stage's end at their full divergence, with their terrain, so the car is always on road with road ahead. Over the last 160 m before the end, the world hazes out: the fog thickens toward a white-out, and the painted sky, the distance and the clouds fade into it. The stage changes inside the haze, and the next stage clears out of it over its first 240 m. The haze is a pure function of the stage and the car's place in it, so a replay or a scrub shows the same. The owner: "the stage end has not improved, it is still driving into the void." The change does what it can ahead of time:
- The next stages' clouds are painted while the current stage is driven.
- The sky's light and the image settings are swapped without dirtying every material.
- Traffic cars are pooled.
- The verge builds one piece a frame inside the haze.

The game stays hidden behind its loading line until its first frame is drawn. The owner saw "a ghost version of the car before the controls appear".

## Rival

Each stage names one of the three AI cars as its rival. It starts ahead of the player at the checkpoint; a glass callout under the timer shows its name, the gap in seconds and a closing bar. Passing it before the next checkpoint pays 1,000 and flashes RIVAL BEATEN in the attention green.

## Hero car

Seen from the chase camera, the hero car is a late-1980s two-seat convertible with no badges: segmented red and amber tail lights in black housings over a blank plate, chrome twin exhausts, side strakes, pop-up headlamp lids, an open cockpit and five-spoke wheels that spin and steer. It is the art pack's `model/car-hero`, a Hunyuan3D-2 body from an SDXL reference with materials cut from its geometry; every car shares it and takes its character's paint. It is a front-engined GT shape rather than the original's low mid-engined wedge. Its body is faired smooth, its wheel arches cut as true circles, its colour blocks bordered on clean curves, its tyres round, and its paint a soft clearcoat: a toy car rather than a scan. The traffic set shares the treatment. On the road its nose points along its own path, a few degrees off the road for a lane change (at most about 7°), and its body leans a little out of the move. Steering is the kinematic bicycle model racing games build on: the stick turns the front wheels, the car yaws at v·tan(δ)/L on a 2.4 m wheelbase, and it moves sideways along its own heading, so a lane change is an arc rather than a slide. The wheels ramp in over about 170 ms and return over about 100 ms. Their reach shrinks with speed, as arcade racers do, so a tap at 280 km/h is a correction of a few centimetres rather than a lunge; and self-aligning torque straightens the car once the stick is released. The body rides damped springs over wheels that stay on the road, as a raycast vehicle's sprung mass does over its unsprung wheels: it squats a little as it pulls away and dives a little under braking, rolls out of a lane change and back once as the car straightens, and settles. The angles are a road car's, about 2.5–3° per g and at most about a degree, and the dampers are a sports car's (about 1.4 Hz, damping ratio 0.7), so a move settles with one small overshoot. An animator's anticipation, a lean into the move before it, was tried and dropped: driven by how fast the wheel turned, it rocked the body on every tap of the stick, and with the wheels riding the body the car read as round rather than planted. Nitro's kick stretches it a few percent along its length, keeping its volume, and it springs back. The springs run in the simulation's own step, so a replay or a scrub poses the body the same. On nitro a jet burns from each side's exhaust, yellow-white at the pipe and burning out orange. It rides the car, and each particle stays under the bloom threshold so only the dense core glows: flames left behind in the world drew a red ball per frame at speed. When the burst ends nothing stops dead: the extra speed bleeds off over about a second and a half (and running wide onto the grass brakes the car hard over a second rather than stopping it in a frame), the flame thins out over half a second, and the camera eases back in over about a second.

## Chase camera

The chase camera breathes with the race rather than holding one offset. At rest it frames the car as the Ghibli concepts do (the owner chose their composition on `feat/apex-ghibli`): 7.5 m behind and 3 m up on a long lens (0.68 rad high at 16:9, where the close chase had 0.85), the car small at the foot of the frame, its tail about a quarter of the width, the road running to a horizon low enough that the sky takes 35 to 40% of the frame. A screen narrower than 16:9, a phone held upright, keeps the 16:9 frame's width in view and opens up and down instead of cropping to its middle. It pulls back as speed builds and well back on nitro, so the whole car and its flame show, and a car or rival within about 90 m draws it in toward the hood, for concentration; only nitro pushes it back. It eases in over a few seconds, so a car passing by draws it part way and only one kept alongside takes it all the way onto the hood, and it lets go slower still, over several seconds once the road is clear. There is no camera switch: the hood view is a moment of the chase camera rather than a mode. Steering, it stays behind the car and looks a little along the car's heading, so a turn rotates the view rather than sliding the car across the frame. Coming up to a crest where the road falls away it lifts and aims down, so the drop shows before the car goes over. A landmark ahead draws it well up and wide while the landmark stands in the frame ahead: 7.8 m further back, 3.9 m higher, 2.7 m out to the landmark's side and its lens 0.094 rad wider at full pull, about doubling its resting distance and height (the owner found a pull of 6 and 2.6 m "a bit too subtle and a bit too short", and 10 and 5 m "a bit too much"; on the long lens the close chase's pull read as half the move), building from 620 m out, full from 460 m to 80 m, and gone 60 m past (fullest beside the landmark, it framed the road while the landmark left the frame). For the stage's hero landmark the camera also turns toward it, its aim 47% of the way from the road to the landmark's middle height at full pull, so it stands in the frame beside the road as the camera pulls back; the fork's checkpoint arch and the rarer set pieces (no two moments within 900 m, none in the first 400 m) only draw it up and wide, swinging out to the landmark's side. Every term but the hood's and nitro's eases is a smooth function of the race state, so a replay or a scrub frames nearly the same shot.

## Telemetry HUD

Glass panels on the four corners: stage and fork progress, remaining time with checkpoint extensions, the Sega route matrix and radio, highway telemetry, and transmission with a large velocity readout. The HUD splits by refresh rate (pronto's kinetic-machine record): the panels, labels and what changes with the stage are the page's; what moves with the frame — speed, revs, tachometer, gear, nitro, timer, score, route progress, fork distance, radar, g-force, slipstream, draft meter, equalizer and the rival's gap — is drawn by the renderer's worker into canvases set where those digits sat, in the font, colour and glow the panel gives them, so it reads as before. What changes at a milestone — the stage's number and name, the fork's two labels, the route matrix, the rival card's words, the title, pause and results cards, the initials and the board — is the page's, over the run row the race's milestones write; the stage named changes as the arch is passed, before the haze. The moments timed to the frame — the stage card's swoop, 3-2-1 and GO!, GOAL! or TIME OVER, the stage-clear card, the close-pass and rival banners, the haze and the road trip's letterbox and titles — are the worker's too, painted over the panels (the haze under them) as their CSS looked. On the Ghibli branch the painting is the screen: the panels are drawn at four fifths of their size and half see-through. On a phone, landscape or upright, only the race's own readouts stay, at three fifths: the stage card, the time, the rival and the speed; the telemetry, the radio, the route matrix and the surface strip go.

## The renderer's thread

The world is painted off the page's thread, by the renderer as a presenter (pronto's endowed-Babylon record): a worker that draws each frame on a canvas it was handed and can reach nothing else. The look is the same picture: shots at fixed ticks stay inside the main-thread build's own run-to-run noise. Frames hold better: about 20 intervals over 25 ms in 20 s of the first stage, against about 200 when the page drew them. The adaptive resolution scales the canvas's CSS size, never its last drawing size, so a slow stretch steps the pixels down and back up without compounding.

## The simulation's cage

The race steps in the terminal's cage, not as page script: the simulation, its PRNG and the stage layout it places props by are a Jessie module graph (`program.cue` `surface.engines.apexrun`), linked when the app is generated and imported by an SES compartment endowed with nothing. The renderer's worker and the HUD import the same files as ordinary modules, so the tables they read are the simulation's own. A step costs about 110 µs in the cage against 45 µs as page script, under a hundredth of a frame. The run's milestones — its grid, its start, each stage's landmark ahead, each checkpoint, time over, the finish and a duel's finish — are lifted out of the race once their frame is confirmed, each with the commands since the last, two bytes a frame, a landmark's with the frame's state too; with the seed, they replay the run to any milestone, which is how a high score will be checked and how scrubbing the rows' history will reach the race. The race plays the same in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit; Firefox draws it with WebGPU, WebKit (without WebGPU on this Mac) with WebGL2.

## Duel

Two people race one stage (pronto's kinetic-machine record): a car each from the countdown, the stage's traffic and no scripted rival, and the first car under the arch wins; at time over the one farther along does. Both cars race on the hero car's numbers, and each player sees their own as the hero car and the other's as the opponent's, whose gap the rival card reads in seconds. A fork is never taken: two players on two stages share no road. The title card's DUEL button (or the 2 key) puts both on one keyboard, WASD and the arrows, the screen seen from the first seat.

Online, ONLINE DUEL opens the lobby, a card of the title's own glass: the racer's callsign, a switch to go online (attention green once on), the ten road stages to challenge on (the picked one ringed in the sun's gold), the racers online each with a CHALLENGE button, and challenges in and out as gold-edged rows with ACCEPT, DECLINE or CANCEL. An accepted challenge starts the duel on both screens, each seen from its own seat, raced over a data channel with GGRS predicting the other car's input and rolling back when it arrives. Pause holds an online race for no one. The end card adds the result as the duel machine recorded it — RESULT RECORDED · YOU WON, YOU LOST or DRAW, DISPUTED when the two races disagree, ABANDONED when a racer fell silent — and BACK TO THE LOBBY in place of TRY AGAIN, since an online duel is raced once.

## Palette

Light is the sunlit sand of Coconut Beach: paper-warm neutrals, ink text, a burnt-orange accent taken from the sunset band, rumble red for danger and palm green for attention. Dark is the glass of the telemetry panels at night: blue-black surfaces with the sun's gold as the accent. Stage colours live in `shell/engine/stage_look.js`, not here; these tokens serve the shell and the HUD.

Scenery, compared on 2026-10-16 on the design canvas (https://claude.ai/artifact/Ko3QBgcYMXzxMMBvXhFP44) against the Stitch frame and the turn-6 game: A, everything procedural 3D (shader sky, fractal ridges, lofted palms); B, a generated painted panorama with sprite palms in the 1986 arcade manner; C, a hybrid of the two. Toward 2025-era graphics, 2026-10-16, in conversation after turn 8. The owner wants **modern lighting** and a **WebGPU** renderer. Assets will **not be committed**: CC0 sky maps and texture sets are fetched at build time, pinned by URL and checksum, and what the free libraries lack (palm variants, rocks, roadside props) is **AI-generated by open models on our own GPU**, pinned by prompt, seed and model version and cached by checksum. Rejected: generation in the player's browser (seconds to minutes per asset and gigabytes of weights), and a hosted generation API (vendor lock-in and per-asset cost). The "no downloaded art" rule of turn 7 is lifted only in this form: the art is reproducible from recipes, not stored in git.

Lighting, built in turn 9 and played on an Apple GPU under WebGPU. Ambient occlusion (SSAO2) was tried and dropped: the sky is a custom shader that writes none of the prepass buffers SSAO reads, so it rendered the sky black. Motion blur was tried and dropped: screen-based blur smears the player's car, which moves with the camera, and object-based blur needs a velocity target that, next to the HDR and reflection targets, exceeds Apple's 32 bytes per sample. Temporal anti-aliasing overflows the same budget, so anti-aliasing is MSAA plus FXAA. The point lights for the headlight and nitro were removed: with shadows they pushed shaders past the 16 fragment inputs Apple allows.

Art pack, 2026-10-17, in conversation after turn 13. A one-off trial on the local GPU showed the props; the owner chose to see "the limit we can push this" and to judge it in the game, with no limit on offline generation time. Decided by the owner: the game reads every asset from the **blob service** (`/blobs/mecha-objects/<sha256>.<ext>`), which a launch fills from a **public-read GCS origin** (`gs://trash-362115-supertuxkart-assets`), each file refused unless it hashes to its name. Git holds the manifest, the recipes and the tools, never the bytes. Rejected: a local volume only (a fresh machine has no art for hours), Git LFS (the committed-assets path ruled out after turn 8), and generating in the player's browser or per play (gigabytes of weights, minutes per asset). CC0 photoscans are used wherever they exist; the generated models are the palms, the traffic bodies, the cactus and the columns. ACES tone mapping, dropped in turn 7 against the shader sky, is on: the photographed skies are calibrated for it.

Graphics packs, built in turn 11. Backlit fronds, wind sway, sun-tinted haze, a lens flare, a reflection probe on the car, the speed feel and night headlight pools shipped. Ambient occlusion was tried again and dropped. Through the prepass it reads the custom sky as fully occluded (turn 9). Through a separate geometry buffer, Babylon 7.54.3 under WebGPU leaves a texture binding undefined and the first frame throws. Bringing it back needs a sky that writes the prepass buffers, or a Babylon version whose geometry-buffer path works on WebGPU. The haze is sun-tinted distance fog; true height fog would need a depth-based pass, and was not built.

Screen-space reflections were dropped after the owner played turn 11. In the hood camera they traced rays off the clear-coated hood and the terrain right at the eye, and the misses drew stair-stepped black blocks that flashed as the car moved. The probe that follows the car carries its reflections instead.

Babylon 9.31.0, 2026-10-17. The owner agreed to try a newer Babylon to bring back ambient occlusion. Apexrun moved from 7.54.3 to 9.31.0, pinned with integrity. The shared CSP admits both versions, so the other Babylon apps (doom, supertux) are unchanged. On 9.31.0 SSAO2 works in both modes under WebGPU, with the sky intact, so ambient occlusion is back on WebGPU through its own geometry buffer. The upgrade also exposed a turn-11 bug: the wind plugin's GLSL never declared its uniform, so on WebGL2 its shader failed to compile.
