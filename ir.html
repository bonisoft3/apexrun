Apex Run — IR (Architecture & Contracts)

Status: Pinned architecture for the Out Run highway simcade on Pronto.

1. Topology & Planes

  • Kinetic Fast Plane (60Hz): Fixed-step deterministic highway physics loop: drifting, nitro, traffic, forks, the stage clock, and a circular rollback history ring.
  • Relational State Plane (Durable): One live, public-read high-score table; the kart game's Match, Kart and LapRecord stay only as retired identities.
  • Presentation: a full-frame Babylon.js WebGPU canvas with the glass telemetry HUD, and a pit-lane telemetry overlay (~) with the rollback host's rewind scrubber.

the platform’s columns

created_by, updated_by → app_user, stamped by the platform on every write; not the author’s.

One shared online table of finished runs, read live by every player. Public to read; a player inserts rows of their own and nobody edits or removes one (decision-leaderboard-writes). The engine's clock fixes what a run can claim: it opens at 75 s and each of the four checkpoints adds 45 s, so a destination is reached at stage 5 with clock left, and the clock runs out at stage s exactly when 75 + 45(s-1) s are spent. The score's ceiling is every score line at its most over the whole 255 s clock.

A letter is stage 5 with run_ms under 255000; time_over at stage s has run_ms exactly 75000 + 45000(s-1).

The top ten read orders by score descending, then run_ms ascending, then id, so equal runs keep one order on every screen.

7. Architectural Decisions

  • The world is real 3D in Babylon.js on WebGPU: road, terrain, props and cars are meshes lit and painted by the gouache passes. Rejected: Out Run's pseudo-3D road projection, which holds no hills to stand props on, no camera moves and no painted models.
  • 60Hz deterministic kinetic simulation conforms to Pronto algebraic isomorphism contracts with bit-exact rollback history.
  • Input is omnishell's createKineticInput, over its vendored game-inputs, with dual code/key matching and the Gamepad API.
  • The high-score table is public to read with its writes added in a raw migration, because the access model has no "everyone reads, the owner inserts" mode. The insert arm checks the row's player against the session; there is no update or delete arm and the privileges are revoked, so a score is a record and not a draft. A BEFORE INSERT trigger stamps the server's clock and admits one row per player per 75 s, the shortest any run can last; a redelivered insert carries the id it was minted with and passes the trigger untouched, so the gateway's ignore-duplicates absorbs it. Rejected: declaring the table private and widening its read in SQL, which would make it "mine" to the browser and "everyone's" to the server; and a per-run token from a server function, which an anonymous arcade does not need and which cannot make a modified client honest.
  • Escape hatches: the art pack seed (hatch-assets), and the renderer (renderer), a presenter whose worker paints the road and the HUD's fast readouts into the screen's canvas layers and does nothing else. The simulation is not a hatch: it is a Jessie engine (shell/engine/engine.js and the modules it imports) that the terminal's cage steps, endowed with nothing, and it lifts the run's milestones (grid, start, checkpoint, time over, finish) with the commands since the last. The sound is a Jessie role (shell/engine/sound.js) on the AudioContext the terminal tames. The race is the arena's Cortex kinetic child (pronto's kinetic-machine record): the terminal starts it on the screen's mount, and the run machine (shell/handlers/run.js, Jessie) folds what it lifts and what is pressed into the run row. No script of the app's own is served.
  • The art pack seed. Pronto has no verb for content the app ships but does not commit, so a one-shot container copies the pack into the blob store at launch. Every file is named by its sha256 in assets/manifest.json, fetched from the public origin bucket, and refused unless its bytes hash to that name. The game reads the files at /blobs/mecha-objects/.

8. Units

renderer — the kinetic plane's painter

Babylon.js 9.31 on WebGPU, drawing each frame the kinetic host hands it on a canvas its seat transfers. Babylon cannot run under SES, so the renderer is plain JavaScript in a presenter's worker: its script's policy refuses every connection but blob: and data:, its globals are pruned to what drawing needs, and it raises nothing. Its art arrives through the seat by app path, read while the race holds. It is level 0, a projection with a cache: a frame scrubbed back to is a similar picture, not the same one.
